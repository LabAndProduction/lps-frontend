Where to Set Up the Balance: Anti-Vibration Plate, Draft and Static Protection

Analytical balances are sensitive to three things: vibration, drafts, and static charge. Put the instrument on a rigid anti-vibration plate or a massive table fixed to the wall, not to the shop floor. Nearby there should be no compressors, centrifuges, or doorways with active traffic.

Checks you can do yourself:

  1. Place a weight on the pan and lean your hand on the table: the reading should return to its original value within seconds without jitter.
  2. Turn on the fume hood or air conditioner at working speed: if the reading drifts, move the balance or install a shield.
  3. Wipe the pan with an antistatic cloth and repeat the weighing: a difference in the third digit indicates accumulated charge.

Keep room humidity in the 40–60% range and temperature stable. Sudden fluctuations cause zero drift that's easily mistaken for a fault. Balances for pharmaceuticals and balances for chemical labs are placed in separate rooms, away from the weighing area with large samples.

Calibration and Verification of Sartorius Balances: What to Order and How Often

Calibration of laboratory balances confirms that the instrument shows mass within the stated error. Verification is an official procedure with a document issued by accredited organizations. For labs following methods with specified accuracy, verification is often mandatory.

Internal calibration in Sartorius models runs automatically on a schedule or when temperature changes. This maintains stability between external procedures but doesn't replace official verification.

Approximate frequency: external calibration once a year, more often under intensive use or after repair. Schedule verification in advance so you don't stop the method mid-series. Requirements for non-automatic weighing instruments are described in OIML R 76, and the manufacturer provides installation and calibration recommendations.

Calculating Sample Size with the Balance in Mind: How Not to Ruin the Analysis

0.1 mg readability doesn't mean you can weigh any sample in that range with that accuracy. Analytical balances have a minimum working weight: below it, relative error grows quickly. For most 0.1 mg class models, the minimum sample at which deviation stays within 0.1–0.2% is around 10–20 mg. Weigh 5 mg of standard and a 0.1 mg error gives 2% scatter, enough to fail method validation.

Estimating the allowable sample is easy: divide readability by the required relative error. For 0.1 mg and 0.1% error, the minimum is 100 mg. If the method calls for less, choose a balance with 0.01 mg steps or prepare a standard solution with subsequent dilution. The reverse rule for the upper limit: the heaviest container plus sample must not exceed the maximum load with a 10–20% margin.

You need to prepare 100 mL of a 1 mg/mL solution, meaning weigh 100 mg of substance with 0.1% accuracy. A 100 mg sample with 0.1 mg readability gives exactly 0.1% relative error—that's borderline. Choose a balance with 0.01 mg steps: the same sample gives 0.01%, and the method passes with margin. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between analytical and precision balances?

Analytical balances work with 0.1 mg readability and have a draft shield; precision balances have steps from 1 mg and usually no shield. The former are for standards and microsamples, the latter for medium-mass samples and reagents.

Do Sartorius scales need to be verified?

If your method specifies weighing accuracy or your lab is pursuing accreditation, verification is mandatory. The instrument's internal calibration doesn't replace it. To arrange the paperwork, check the requirements with your supplier when you place your order.

How often should scales be calibrated?

A good rule of thumb is once a year under normal use, and more often with heavy use, after repairs, or after relocation. The exact schedule depends on the requirements of your method.

Which accessories should I get first?

A weight set for external calibration, a spare pan, and antistatic wipes. For data transfer, a cable that matches the port you need.

Can I place the scales by a window?

No — direct sunlight and drafts from the window cause the readings to drift. Choose a spot away from doors, air conditioners, and vibrations.
